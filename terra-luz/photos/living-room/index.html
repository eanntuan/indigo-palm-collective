TERRA LUZ

Living Room

Rattan, warmth, books. Nothing staged.

The living room was the last to come together, which probably explains why it feels the most natural. We swapped in a walnut TV stand Dawn approved in March, kept the original jute rug because it earned its place, and added two faux fan palms in blue pots. Dawn specifically called for blue to balance the terracotta and the warm wood tones. She was right.

The bookcase holds actual books, not staged filler, and the large guitar player artwork, a piece we found on Etsy, fills its wall without asking for attention, which is harder to pull off than it sounds.

This is the room everyone ends up in after dark: cards on the coffee table, a movie nobody's fully watching, a drink resting on one of the recycled Mexican tile coasters we picked out instead of whatever plain cork set came with the furniture. Small detail, but it's the kind of thing that tells you someone thought about this room instead of just filling it.

The rattan seating came from a combination of sourced pieces and what was already working. Nothing in this room came from a big-box matching set. The result is a room that feels collected over time, like a house where people actually live rather than a set dressed for a shoot. You will sit down here and not want to move until dinner.

Plantation shutters cut the desert sun into stripes across the sectional, and somebody always ends up napping under the rattan pendant (myself included!).

A jute rug marks off the sectional corner on tile that forgives wet pool feet.

Movie night gets a proper stage: a terracotta lime-wash alcove, with faux palm trees framing the 75 inch Roku Smart TV.

A guitarist in a wide terracotta hat holds court over the sectional corner, a postcard of an arched courtyard hung nearby like a souvenir.

Terracotta lime-wash gives the TV nook its glow, with a cane-front console beneath and a tall faux palm in a blue pot that never asks for water.

Peek down the hall: blue tropical wallpaper through the first doorway, terracotta tile floors in the room at the far end, and a stack of striped towels on the cabinets in between.

From the sectional to the patio door it is one open room, so the whole group never gets separated, unless they want to bet :P

Light through the shutters lands on the Living Spaces table and cane-back chairs. Overhead fan to keep the air moving.

The indoor/outdoor flow of the patio and living room is one of my favorite parts of the home.
