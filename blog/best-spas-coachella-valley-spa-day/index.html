6 Best Spas in the Coachella Valley: Prices & How to Book

By Eann Tuan · July 11, 2026 · 9 min read

Palm Springs exists because of a hot spring. The Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla Indians have used the mineral water under what's now downtown for centuries, and the resort town that grew up around it never really let go of the spa habit. That history means the valley has more legitimate day-spa options than most desert destinations, where "spa" usually means a hotel amenity locked behind a room key.

Not every resort spa here takes walk-ins or day visitors. Some are guests-only, full stop, no matter how good the marketing photos look. Below are the ones that sell a day pass to the public, what they cost, and what you're getting for it. Prices and hours shift with season and demand, so call or check the booking page before you drive out.

The Spa at Séc-hé, Downtown Palm Springs

500 E Amado Rd, Palm Springs. This is the one with the actual mineral hot springs, the reason the whole city is here. It's open to the public with no hotel stay required, which is rare for a spa this good. A day pass runs somewhere in the $125-$165 range depending on the day of the week, with weekends priced higher than weekdays. The hydrotherapy circuit, salt caves, and private mineral soak option are the draw, along with the "Earthing Zone" loungers if you just want to sit and do nothing for an hour.

Reservations are recommended, especially on weekends when locals and visitors both try to get in. This is the pick if you want one spa experience that's specifically about being in Palm Springs, not interchangeable with a spa day anywhere else.

Two Bunch Palms, Desert Hot Springs

67425 Two Bunch Palms Trail, Desert Hot Springs, about 20 minutes north of downtown Palm Springs. This is an adults-only property built around a natural mineral spring that's been in continuous use for over a century, with six private outdoor soaking tubs and 19 treatment rooms spread across a quiet, low-key compound. Day visitors are welcome, but reservations are required and the property does not admit children even with a day pass. Hours run Monday through Thursday 10am to 6pm, Friday through Sunday 10am to 7pm.

Old Hollywood used this place specifically because it's quiet and private, and that character has held up. If Séc-hé is the lively downtown version of a mineral soak, Two Bunch Palms is the version where nobody talks to you.

Well Spa+Salon at La Quinta Resort & Club

49-499 Eisenhower Drive, La Quinta. The spa building here is original to the resort's 1926 opening, Spanish-colonial architecture with tile work and courtyards that don't feel retrofitted for a modern spa the way a lot of resort spas do. A day facility pass runs around $65 per person, though that covers spa access only, not the resort's separate pool areas. There's an outdoor relaxation space with a fireplace that's worth the price on its own in the cooler months.

Reservations are required and availability moves around based on resort occupancy. This one's the pick if you're staying in or near La Quinta and want something with more architectural character than a standard resort spa.

JW Marriott Desert Springs Resort & Spa, Palm Desert

74855 Country Club Drive, Palm Desert. At 38,000 square feet this is the largest hotel spa in the Greater Palm Springs area, and it's built for volume without feeling like it. A full-day pass runs about $125, with a half-day pass around $45 if you just want a couple hours. The aromatic hammam, Finnish sauna, and Turkish steam room give it more range than most valley spas, and the "Mind Sync Chairs" are a genuinely odd, genuinely good addition if you've never tried one.

Book through the spa's own site, Marriott's iDayPass program, or ResortPass. This is the pick if you want the most amenities per dollar and don't need the intimacy of a smaller property.

Santé Spa at Riviera Resort & Spa, Palm Springs

1600 N Indian Canyon Drive, Palm Springs. The largest resort spa in Palm Springs proper, with 18 treatment rooms and a Blue Haven Lounge that includes a Watsu pool, shaded cabanas, and separate men's and women's steam rooms. Non-guests get in through ResortPass rather than booking direct in most cases. Exact day-pass pricing moves with demand, so check the ResortPass listing before you plan around it.

This one sits closer to downtown than JW Marriott Desert Springs or Well Spa+Salon, so it's the practical choice if you're based in Palm Springs proper and don't want to drive toward La Quinta or Palm Desert.

Estrella Spa at Avalon Hotel & Bungalows, Palm Springs

415 S Belardo Road, Palm Springs. Smaller and more design-forward than the resort spas above, with a midcentury-modern building and a complimentary steam room included in the spa amenity pass, priced around $75 per person for up to four hours of access. The caveat: the pool stays guests-only even with the spa pass, and the spa runs appointment-only hours, 9am to 4pm. Reservations required.

Worth it if the architecture and quiet scale matter more to you than pool access, but go in knowing the pool isn't part of the deal.

What to Skip (or Book Differently)

Sensei Porcupine Creek in Rancho Mirage doesn't sell a standalone day pass. Spa access there comes bundled into overnight stays only, with spa credit built into the room rate, and the property is adults-only. If a Sensei spa day is the goal, an overnight stay is the actual product, not a side option.

Parker Palm Springs is similar: the pool stays guests-only, and non-guests can book spa treatments directly but not a facility day pass. A few reviews describe an unwelcoming front-desk experience for walk-in non-guests, so if you're set on the Parker, book a specific treatment in advance rather than showing up expecting general spa access.

Making the Most of a Spa Day Out Here

Book ahead. Every spa on this list requires or strongly recommends a reservation, and weekend slots at Séc-hé and Two Bunch Palms fill fastest. Weekday visits are both cheaper and easier to book at almost every property on this list.

Hydrate more than usual before you go. Mineral soaks, steam rooms, and hammams pull water out of you fast in desert heat, and the dry air here makes dehydration sneak up quicker than it would at a spa somewhere humid. Bring a water bottle even to places that provide their own.

Pair it with the rest of the day. Well Spa+Salon sits inside La Quinta Resort, a few minutes from the shops on Old Town La Quinta. Santé Spa and Estrella Spa are both close enough to downtown Palm Springs to combine with lunch on Palm Canyon Drive. None of these require building an entire day around the spa alone.

Where to Stay for a Spa-Focused Trip

The Sundune in Palm Springs puts you closest to Séc-hé, Santé Spa, and Estrella Spa, all within a short drive of downtown. If you'd rather base out toward Indio, Terra Luz and The Cozy Cactus are both closer to La Quinta and Palm Desert, putting Well Spa+Salon and JW Marriott Desert Springs within 20 minutes. Frequently Asked Questions

Which Palm Springs-area spa is open to the public without a hotel stay?

The Spa at Séc-hé in downtown Palm Springs is the most reliable public option, with mineral hot springs and no room requirement. Two Bunch Palms in Desert Hot Springs also takes day visitors, though reservations are required and children aren't admitted even with a day pass.

Do any Coachella Valley spas require a hotel stay to use?

Yes. Sensei Porcupine Creek bundles spa access into overnight stays only, with no standalone day pass. Parker Palm Springs keeps its pool guests-only and generally books non-guests for individual treatments rather than full facility access.

How much does a spa day pass cost in the Coachella Valley?

Prices range from around $45 for a half-day pass at JW Marriott Desert Springs to $125-$165 for a full day at Séc-hé on a weekend. Well Spa+Salon at La Quinta Resort runs about $65 for facility access alone. Prices shift with season and day of week, so confirm current pricing before booking.

Do I need a reservation for a spa day, or can I walk in?

Reservations are required or strongly recommended at every spa on this list. Weekend slots at the most popular spots, especially Séc-hé and Two Bunch Palms, book up in advance. Walking in without a reservation is a real risk of getting turned away.

Which spa has actual natural hot springs, not just heated pools?

The Spa at Séc-hé sits on the Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla Indians' mineral hot springs, the same water source that gave Palm Springs its name. Two Bunch Palms in Desert Hot Springs draws from a natural mineral spring that's been used for more than a century.
