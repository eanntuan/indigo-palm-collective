How to Write an Airbnb Listing Description That Books

By Eann Tuan · July 11, 2026 · 6 min read

Most Airbnb listing descriptions fail for the same reason. They answer "what does this place have?" when the guest is asking something else: "is this right for my group?" Those are two very different questions, and the listing that answers the second one books at a higher rate.

We run four properties across the Coachella Valley and Palm Springs under Indigo Palm Collective: Terra Luz, The Cozy Cactus, The Sundune, and The Well. Between them, we have 191 reviews and a collective rating that stays above 4.9. What four years of guest feedback taught us is that the details guests respond to in person are the same details they respond to in the listing. The description either earns trust before they book, or it doesn't.

Here's the five-section formula we use. It's not complicated. It's just aimed at the right question.

Section 1: Who This Is For

The first two or three sentences of your listing are doing a specific job: helping guests self-select. Not "perfect for everyone" or "ideal for families and groups." Those mean nothing. Something specific enough that the right guest recognizes themselves immediately.

For Terra Luz, we open with something like: "Terra Luz is for groups who care as much about the interior as the pool. The people who send kitchen photos before they unpack." That sentence loses some guests and attracts others, which is exactly the point. The wrong guests leaving is better than the wrong guests staying and leaving a 3-star review.

For The Cozy Cactus, our opener is more direct: if your group has kids under 10 and someone needs a pack-and-play, this is the property. Not "family-friendly," which could mean anything. Specific enough to be useful.

Section 2: The One Thing You Do Better Than Anything Nearby

Not a list of amenities. One specific differentiator. This is the hardest section to write because it requires you to know what makes your property different, not just what it has.

At Terra Luz, the Wild Tropics bedroom earns its own sentence in the description. Canopy bed, ceiling art, guests photograph it within the first hour of arrival, every single stay. We mention it by name and describe it briefly. That one room has driven bookings we know came directly from guests who saw it in photos and then confirmed it in the listing copy.

For The Cozy Cactus, the differentiator isn't a room. It's the operational detail: labeled drawers, a Stokke high chair, a game room that functions as a game room. The property works for families, not just photographs well for them. That distinction matters to parents who've been burned by "family-friendly" listings that turned out to be decorated adult spaces with one token high chair.

For The Sundune in Palm Springs, it's being inside the city instead of out at the valley edge. The Uptown Design District is close enough to walk for coffee and shops, and downtown Palm Canyon Drive is a ten-minute drive rather than a half-hour commute in from Indio or Cathedral City. Most desert rentals at this price sit much farther out, so I say the drive time plainly instead of implying you can walk to dinner.

One specific claim is more convincing than ten generic ones. Pick your differentiator and commit to it.

Section 3: The Practical Specifics

This is the section guests scan when they're comparing two properties side by side. Bullet format works here. Dense prose does not.

Cover the basics: beds, baths, max occupancy, parking situation, pool type (private vs. shared, heated vs. not), pet policy, checkout time, and what access guests get. If there are restrictions that matter, say them here. A guest who discovers a pet policy surprise on arrival is worse than a guest who self-filtered at the listing stage.

For Terra Luz: 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, sleeps 8, private Kahlo blue saltwater pool with a heated spa (spa heating included, pool heating $75/night), private driveway parking, dog-friendly with prior approval. For The Cozy Cactus: 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, sleeps 8, private hot tub plus access to three heated community pools at Indian Palms Country Club, game room, pack-and-play available, not pet-friendly. For The Sundune: 2 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, three king beds, sleeps 6, balcony with views, assigned parking, shared HOA pool, ten minutes from downtown Palm Springs.

Don't bury these details in paragraphs. Guests skim this section, not read it.

Section 4: Location With Specifics, Not Distances

Named landmarks convert. Vague proximity claims don't.

"Walking distance to the Empire Polo Club" tells a Coachella or Stagecoach guest everything they need to know. "Close to the festival grounds" tells them nothing, because every property in the valley says that. Both Terra Luz and The Cozy Cactus are walking distance to the Empire Polo Club, where Coachella and Stagecoach are held, and we say that plainly. No drive times, no mileage estimates. Walking distance.

For The Sundune, we name the Uptown Design District shops you can reach on foot and give the honest drive time to Palm Canyon Drive, instead of writing "close to dining" and hoping nobody checks. Guests who've been burned by "close to everything" listings that turned out to be a 15-minute drive know exactly how to read vague copy, so specifics signal that you know the area and aren't guessing.

Apply this standard to every proximity claim in your description. If you can't name the landmark, you don't really know how close you are to it.

Section 5: Why Book Direct

Airbnb charges guests a 20% service fee on the booking subtotal. On a $1,250 stay, that's $250 added on before taxes. Booking directly through our site at indigopalm.co removes that fee entirely. The guest keeps $250. We get paid the same. Airbnb gets nothing from the transaction.

Most guests don't know this is an option. We tell them in the listing. Not aggressively, not in a way that violates Airbnb's terms, but as a plain fact near the end of the description. Something like: "We also accept direct bookings at indigopalm.co, which skips the Airbnb service fee." That sentence earns more direct bookings per year than any other single line of copy we've tested. If you want to understand the math on what guests save, we've broken it down in full.

What Not to Include

Don't list every appliance model. Don't count pillows. Don't open with your origin story unless it directly affects the guest experience, and even then, keep it to two sentences.

Guests reading your listing aren't auditing an inventory. They're deciding if the vibe matches their group. A description that sounds like a spec sheet signals that the property is run by someone who doesn't understand what guests care about. Save the full inventory for the house rules and welcome guide, where it belongs and where guests will use it.

Write for the decision, not the documentation. Five sections, each aimed at one question. That's the formula.
