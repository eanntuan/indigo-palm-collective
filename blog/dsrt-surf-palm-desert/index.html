DSRT Surf Palm Desert: Prices, Hours + What to Know

By Eann Tuan · October 6, 2026 · 6 min read

A surf lagoon opened between two golf courses in Palm Desert on September 26, which is a sentence I never expected to write about the desert (and yet here we are).

I'll be upfront: I haven't surfed it yet. Everything below comes from the local press and a development tracker, both linked at the bottom, and I'll flag what nobody has confirmed. Once I've been, I'll update this with the part only a person who got wet can tell you.

What DSRT Surf Is

DSRT Surf sits on a 17.8-acre site inside Desert Willow Golf Resort, wedged between two of the resort's courses. The lagoon holds about 9 million gallons and runs on Wavegarden Cove technology, which the operators bill as the largest system of its kind in North America.

The pitch is a menu. Before each session the wave gets picked: soft rollers for beginners, long intermediate walls, or barrels for people who already know what they're doing. The lagoon splits into two sections of up to 28 surfers each, and some sessions (the "pro barrel" ones) cap the headcount lower.

The DSRT Surf lagoon from above, sitting between two Desert Willow golf courses. Photo: Surfer.

Prices and Hours

The entry point is the Learn-to-Surf session at $100, or $75 if you live in the Coachella Valley, and it covers 30 minutes on land plus 60 minutes in the water with a coach. Bigger-wave sessions run about $290 an hour, barrel sessions about $370 to $380, and weekday slots come in roughly $20 cheaper.

If you have $19,000 an hour lying around, you can rent the whole pool with left and right waves both running. (I do not.)

Reported hours are Monday to Thursday 8am to 10pm and Friday to Sunday 6am to 10pm, with sessions starting every hour. One thing to ignore: an earlier pre-opening notice floated "from $60 per hour," and nothing I found supports that as a current rate.

How It Compares to Palm Springs Surf Club

They're two separate places run by different operators, in different cities. Both use Wavegarden Cove, so the wave technology is a wash and the difference comes down to setting and price.

Palm Springs Surf Club's beginner session runs around $100 as well, and you can read the whole breakdown in my Palm Springs Surf Club review. DSRT's edge on paper is the resident rate and the golf-course backdrop, and the 1,000-waves-an-hour figure the operators quote is worth testing for yourself. Its disadvantage right now is simply that it is new.

A group session at Palm Springs Surf Club. DSRT's two-section lagoon is built for the same format.

What Isn't Confirmed Yet

Only the lagoon and the surf center are open, and the operator says other amenities will arrive over the coming weeks and months. Aerial photos from September show a separate pool, loungers, shade sails, two pickleball courts and a skate area, but I couldn't find a confirmed answer on dining, spectator access, day passes for non-surfers, or parking terms.

Phase two is expected to add a 139-room resort hotel and 57 villas, with no opening date I trust. The operators also claim the first surf lagoon with International Dark Sky certification and a turf-for-surf program aimed at net-zero water use. Those are their claims, and I haven't seen them audited.

Staying Nearby

Our Indio houses sit roughly 25 minutes from Desert Willow, depending on how Highway 111 is behaving, which makes a morning session and an afternoon at the pool a workable day. Terra Luz has its own private pool for the cooldown afterward, and The Cozy Cactus has a shared pool with a private hot tub for sore shoulders.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does DSRT Surf cost?

Learn-to-Surf is $100 general and $75 for Coachella Valley residents. Larger waves run about $290 per hour and barrels about $370 to $380, with weekdays around $20 less.

Where is DSRT Surf?

105 Wave Drive in Palm Desert, inside Desert Willow Golf Resort.

Do you need to know how to surf?

No. The Learn-to-Surf session includes a land lesson and a coach in the water, and the lagoon can run soft beginner waves.

Can you watch without surfing?

Unconfirmed. Spectator access and day passes hadn't been announced as of the last reports, so check with DSRT before bringing a crowd.

Is it the same as Palm Springs Surf Club?

No, they're separate venues in separate cities that happen to use the same wave technology.
