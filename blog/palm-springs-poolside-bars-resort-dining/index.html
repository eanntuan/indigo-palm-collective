6 Palm Springs Resort Pools You Can Access Without a Room

By Eann Tuan · July 22, 2026 · 8 min read

Some afternoons I want the whole resort-pool fantasy, a cold drink handed to me on a lounger, misted air, someone else running the umbrella, without paying three hundred dollars for a room I'm going to leave by noon. Turns out you don't have to. A good chunk of the valley's prettiest hotel pools and poolside kitchens will take you as a walk-in or a day-pass guest, no overnight required.

I run three rentals out here and field this exact question from guests constantly: where can we get the vacation-pool feeling for a day without checking in somewhere. So here's the rundown of resort restaurants and poolside bars around Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley that welcome people who aren't hotel guests, organized by how you get in.

Two quick rules before the list. First, dining and lounging are usually separate. A lot of these restaurants seat anyone off the street, but sitting by the pool on a chaise is a day pass you book ahead. Second, everything below is accurate as of 2026, and resort menus and hours move around more than you'd think, so call the morning you plan to go.

Palm Springs proper

Downtown and the Palm Canyon strip is where the walk-in scene is thickest, mostly because the boutique hotels here built their restaurants to pull in locals, not just guests.

1. Palm Canyon Swim & Social at ARRIVE

Palm Canyon Swim & Social is the adults-only poolside restaurant and bar at the ARRIVE hotel on North Palm Canyon, and it's the lowest-friction option on this whole list. As of 2026 the restaurant is open to the public with no reservation, first come first served, running an all-day brunch menu into mid-afternoon and drinks until late evening. Think green chile smash burger, breakfast burrito, a craft cocktail by the fire pits.

The catch: sitting at the restaurant is walk-in, but swimming or claiming a lounge chair on the pool deck is reserved for hotel guests or the limited day passes they release. If pool time is the goal, call ahead to check pass availability. If you just want a drink in a good-looking courtyard, walk right in.

2. Ace Hotel & Swim Club

The Ace Hotel & Swim Club on East Palm Canyon is the one I point people to when they specifically want the pool day, because the Swim Club day pass exists exactly for non-guests: a dip in the outdoor pool, the hot tub, a lounge chair, and food and drink brought poolside. Kings Highway, the on-site diner, runs all day and takes anyone, and the Amigo Room is the dark little hideaway bar for a cocktail once the sun drops.

As of 2026 there's real programming worth timing a visit around, weekend poolside DJ sessions, Monday karaoke in the Amigo Room, and a recurring bingo night. Day passes and pool-party weekends fill up, so book the pass ahead rather than showing up and hoping.

The whole point of a pool day pass: a lounger, shade, and someone else running the bar.

3. The Colony Club at Colony Palms

If the plan is dinner rather than a swim, The Colony Club inside the Colony Palms Hotel on North Indian Canyon is the dressed-up pick. It's a Michelin Guide restaurant doing a vintage-glamour take on American classics, beef Wellington, chicken pot pie, the works, with tables in the main room or out by the pool and mountains. Fully open to the public and bookable on OpenTable, with hours running breakfast through dinner as of 2026.

Colony Palms also sells a poolside day pass that covers a cushioned chaise and towel service if you want to make an afternoon of it, but the restaurant alone is worth the reservation. This is date-night territory, not flip-flops.

Or skip the day pass entirely. Here's my honest bias: the whole reason I bought places with pools is so my guests don't have to buy a stranger's lounge chair by the hour. Terra Luz in Indio has a private pool that's yours alone, no day pass, no crowd, and spa heating if you want the hot tub going. The Cozy Cactus pairs a shared community pool with a private hot tub in the backyard, and both sit walking distance from the Empire Polo Club for festival weekends. Book direct at indigopalm.co and you skip Airbnb's 20% guest service fee, which on a long weekend is real money back in the taco budget.

The Terra Luz pool. No pass, no reservation, no fighting a stranger for the shaded chair.

Cathedral City

4. Sol y Sombra at the Paloma Resort

A little east of downtown, Sol y Sombra sits in the historic mission-style Adobe at the Paloma Resort in Cathedral City, and it's a fully public restaurant, no resort stay required. The menu is Spanish-leaning tapas and paella built around the valley's own produce, and there's a misted patio for the warmer evenings. As of 2026 it runs dinner Tuesday through Saturday, and it takes reservations on OpenTable or by phone.

This one reads more as a sit-down dinner destination than a poolside lounge, but it scratches the same itch: the resort-dining atmosphere without the room key. Good for a group that wants to graze and split a pitcher of something.

Indian Wells, the closest resort pool to Indio

5. H2O Pool Bar at Renaissance Esmeralda

For anyone staying on the east end of the valley near Indio or Coachella, the Renaissance Esmeralda in Indian Wells is the nearest big-resort pool, roughly 20 to 25 minutes west of our Indio rentals. It's known for a large sandy-beach pool, and the H2O Pool Bar handles cocktails and light poolside fare overlooking the water.

As of 2026 the Esmeralda sells day passes to non-guests through ResortPass and Marriott's own day-pass site, covering pool access, jacuzzis, and lawn games, with cabanas available if you want to book a shaded base camp for the group. It's the closest thing to a real resort pool day within easy reach of Indian Palms.

Poolside drinks are the common thread. Some you buy with a day pass, some you make yourself back at the rental.

The under-the-radar one

6. The Barn Kitchen at Sparrows Lodge

The Barn Kitchen at Sparrows Lodge, a small midcentury property back on East Palm Canyon, is the sleeper pick. As of 2026 the kitchen serves lunch daily with sandwiches, salads, and small bites in a courtyard built around a flamingo-pink-umbrella pool, and on certain nights it does a family-style supper, Chicken Wednesdays and Steak Saturdays being the ones people talk about.

The important caveat: seating for non-hotel guests is limited, so this is the one place on the list where you genuinely have to call ahead and reserve, especially for the family-style dinners. Show up unannounced and you may not get in. Plan it, and it's one of the more charming meals in town.

Not every resort bar is out by the water. Some of the best drinks are in the dark little lounges off the pool deck.

How to pull this off

A few things I've learned sending guests to these places. Book any pool day pass or cabana ahead, ideally days out for a summer weekend, because they cap the number of non-guests and the good spots go first. Check the age policy, since a handful of these pools are adults-only and won't let kids in at all. And separate your two goals in your head: a walk-in lunch is easy and spontaneous, but a lounge-by-the-pool afternoon is a booking.

For more valley food and drink beyond the resorts, my best restaurants in Palm Springs guide and the Palm Springs bars roundup both go deeper. And if you're doing this in July heat, the summer heat activities guide pairs a pool day with the indoor stuff for when it's genuinely too hot to be outside.

The through-line is that you don't need to book a resort to feel like you're staying at one. Buy the day pass, grab the walk-in table, order the coconut cocktail. Then, ideally, come back to a place with a pool that's already yours. If you're staying with us, The Sundune in Palm Springs has the shared HOA pool a short walk from the door, and the Indio houses have their own water. Either way, the desert's whole appeal in summer is spending the day half-submerged.

Where the pool day ends: back at Terra Luz, lanterns on, nobody charging by the hour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you use a Palm Springs resort pool without staying there?

Often, yes, through a day pass. As of 2026, resorts like the Ace Hotel & Swim Club and the Renaissance Esmeralda in Indian Wells sell pool day passes to non-guests, usually booked ahead on ResortPass or the hotel's own site, that get you a lounge chair, pool and hot tub access, and poolside food and drink. Passes and cabanas sell out on summer weekends, so reserve early and confirm the current price and any age limits before you drive over.

Which Palm Springs resort restaurants are open to the public?

Several. As of 2026, Palm Canyon Swim & Social at the ARRIVE hotel takes walk-in diners with no reservation, the Colony Club at Colony Palms is a Michelin-listed restaurant open to the public and bookable on OpenTable, and Sol y Sombra at the Paloma Resort in Cathedral City is a fully public tapas spot. The Barn Kitchen at Sparrows Lodge welcomes non-guests too, though seating is limited, so call ahead. Menus and hours shift, so confirm before you go.

Do you need a reservation for poolside dining in Palm Springs?

It depends on the spot. Palm Canyon Swim & Social at ARRIVE is first come, first served for the restaurant, while the Colony Club and Sol y Sombra both take reservations and fill up on weekends. If your plan involves lounging by the pool rather than just eating, that is a separate day pass you almost always need to book in advance. When in doubt, call the venue the morning you plan to go.

Where can I get the resort pool day near Indio and Coachella?

The closest resort pool experience to Indio is in Indian Wells, about 20 to 25 minutes west, where the Renaissance Esmeralda runs a sandy-beach pool with the H2O Pool Bar and sells day passes to non-guests. If you're staying at one of our Indio rentals near Indian Palms, it's an easy afternoon out, though the private pool at Terra Luz or the shared pool and private hot tub at The Cozy Cactus cover most of the craving without the drive. Book either direct at indigopalm.co to skip Airbnb's 20% guest service fee.
