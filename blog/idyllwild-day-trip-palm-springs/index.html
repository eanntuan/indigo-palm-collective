Palm Springs to Idyllwild: 1 Hour, 30 Degrees Cooler

By Eann Tuan · July 11, 2026 · 10 min read

Somewhere around mile 30 on Highway 74, the ocotillo and creosote just stop. The road keeps climbing through switchbacks cut into granite, and the air coming through the vents goes from July-in-the-desert to cool, and then you're driving through a pine forest like someone flipped a set. That's the whole appeal of Idyllwild in one sentence: it's an hour from the pool and it doesn't feel like the same state.

I get asked about this drive constantly by guests staying at Terra Luz and The Cozy Cactus in July and August, when the desert floor is 112 and everyone's looking for literally anywhere else. Idyllwild is the answer, and it's a real hiking destination, not just a place to sit in the shade and call it a day. Here's what to do once you're up there.

Drive from Palm Springs~45 min–1 hr via Hwy 74 (Pines to Palms Highway) or Hwy 243 from Banning
Drive from Indio~52 miles, roughly 70–80 min via Hwy 74
Best trail, easy legsIdyllwild Park Trail (2.5 mi, no permit)
Best trail, in shapeErnie Maxwell Scenic Trail (5 mi, no permit)
Best trail, ambitiousDevil's Slide to Suicide Rock or San Jacinto Peak (permit + Adventure Pass required)
Eat before you drive backFerro or Cafe Aroma, both in town

Two Ways Up the Mountain

Highway 74, the Pines to Palms Highway, climbs out of Palm Desert and does most of its elevation gain in a handful of dramatic switchbacks with pullouts worth using. This is the route I send guests on, mostly because the views on the way up are half the reason to go. The other option is Highway 243 out of Banning, which comes in from the north side of the mountain and is the faster pick if you're coming from LA rather than the valley. Either way you're looking at a similar mileage, somewhere close to 47 miles, and a drive that goes from flat desert floor to 5,400 feet in about 40 minutes of driving.

Downtown Idyllwild sits at just over 5,400 feet, which is most of why it's 30 degrees cooler than Indio in July. Photo: Todd Phillips / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0.

Bring a jacket even in summer. I know that sounds ridiculous when you just left a pool deck, but mornings up there run cold enough that you'll regret leaving it in the car, and afternoon thunderstorms roll in fast during monsoon season, July through September.

The Trails, By How Much You Want to Suffer

This is a completely different hiking experience than what you'll find down in the canyons. If you've already done the desert oasis trails, Andreas Canyon or Tahquitz Canyon, this is the same mountain range from the other side: pine and cedar instead of fan palms, granite instead of sand, and a 10-degree temperature swing between the trailhead and the ridge. I wrote up the desert-side version in our guide to hiking near Palm Springs if you want both halves of the San Jacinto range covered.

Idyllwild Park Trail (Easy)

A 2.5-mile out-and-back through an honest-to-god old growth section of forest, with sugar pines and incense cedar big enough that you'll want to take a photo next to one for scale. No permit needed, minimal elevation gain, and it starts right in town near the county park. This is the one I send families and anyone who wants "I hiked in Idyllwild" without the quad-burning part.

Ernie Maxwell Scenic Trail (Moderate)

Five miles out and back from Humber Park, with just under 900 feet of gain and a payoff view of Lily Rock (most people call it Tahquitz Rock) that's better than anything you'll get from the road. No permit required here either, which makes it the easiest "real hike" option if you don't want to deal with the wilderness permit system below.

Lily Rock at sunset, the same granite face you'll see from Ernie Maxwell. Photo: K Larmer / Wikimedia Commons, public domain.

Devil's Slide to Suicide Rock or San Jacinto Peak (Hard)

This is the trailhead most serious hikers are driving up for. Devil's Slide climbs from Humber Park to Saddle Junction, about 5.2 miles round trip with roughly 1,700 feet of gain, and from Saddle Junction the trail network splits toward Suicide Rock, Tahquitz Peak's fire lookout, or all the way up to San Jacinto Peak at 10,834 feet if you've got the whole day and started early. Deer Springs Trail is the other route to Suicide Rock, a separate 7.1-mile trailhead off Highway 243 north of town, and it's the one to use if Devil's Slide permits are gone for the day.

Both of these require the permit setup below. Don't skip it: rangers do check, and the fine is not worth the $0 the permit costs you.

The Tram Route, If You Want to Arrive on Foot

There's one more way to get to Idyllwild that has nothing to do with a car. The Palm Springs Aerial Tramway drops you at 8,516 feet, and a 10.6-mile point-to-point trail runs from the tram's Long Valley station down through Saddle Junction into town. It's a serious day, six hours or more, one-way, and you'll need a car shuttled to the Idyllwild end or a very patient friend. Not a casual add-on to a beach day, but worth knowing it exists if you're already staying near The Sundune in Palm Springs and the tram is basically in your backyard.

The tram gets you to 8,516 feet in about 10 minutes. Getting to Idyllwild from there on foot takes the rest of the day.

Permits and Parking, the Part People Skip

Anywhere you park to hike in the San Jacinto Wilderness, you need a National Forest Adventure Pass: $5 for the day or $30 for the year, sold at ranger stations and local vendors around town. Separate from that, most of the trails, Devil's Slide, Deer Springs, Marion Mountain, Fuller Ridge, and a few others, require a free wilderness permit. The Ernie Maxwell and Idyllwild Park trails are the exceptions and don't need one.

Permits are self-issue and available 24/7 at the Idyllwild Ranger Station on Highway 243, at Mount San Jacinto State Park headquarters, or at the Long Valley Ranger Station at the top of the tram. The one catch: from Memorial Day through Labor Day, Devil's Slide runs a weekend quota of 30 day-use permits, and they go fast on Saturday and Sunday mornings. If you're set on that trail on a summer weekend, get there early or go on a weekday instead.

Where to Eat Before You Drive Back Down

You will be hungry, and Idyllwild has more good food than a town this size has any right to. Ferro is the one I'd tell you to reserve ahead for: contemporary Italian, cacio e pepe and a bolognese worth the calories, a patio that fills up fast on weekends with live music.

Idyllwild's small downtown, walkable in about ten minutes, which is exactly enough time to decide where to eat.

Cafe Aroma is the other anchor, a mountain bistro with a wooded patio that's been a local institution long enough to have its own history, and it's dog-friendly if you brought one along. For something faster and less formal, the Lumber Mill Bar and Grill does a burger locals argue about (order the Bigfoot), and Mama's Egg House covers brunch if you're heading up early instead of coming down late.

If you're staying at Terra Luz or The Cozy Cactus in Indio, this whole loop, drive up, hike, eat, drive back, runs about 70 minutes each way on Highway 74, which makes it a genuinely doable day trip without feeling like you spent it all in the car. It's one of the few ways to escape a 110-degree afternoon in the valley without leaving the region entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Idyllwild from Palm Springs?

About 45 minutes to an hour by car, roughly 40 miles depending on the exact route. From Indio it's closer to 52 miles and 70-80 minutes via Highway 74.

Do I need a permit to hike in Idyllwild?

For Devil's Slide, Deer Springs, Marion Mountain, and most of the wilderness trails, yes, a free self-issue wilderness permit plus a $5 daily (or $30 annual) National Forest Adventure Pass for parking. Ernie Maxwell Scenic Trail and Idyllwild Park Trail don't require the wilderness permit.

Can I do Idyllwild as a day trip without hiking at all?

Yes. The town itself is worth a few hours: walkable shops, a couple of galleries, Ferro or Cafe Aroma for a meal, and views of Lily Rock from the middle of the street. Plenty of people make the drive up just for lunch and a walk around.

Is Idyllwild cooler than Palm Springs in summer?

Substantially. At roughly 5,400 feet, Idyllwild runs 25-30 degrees cooler than the desert floor most summer afternoons, which is the entire reason this drive is worth making in July and August.

What's the easiest trail in Idyllwild for beginners?

Idyllwild Park Trail. It's 2.5 miles, minimal elevation gain, no permit required, and starts within walking distance of downtown.

If Idyllwild is your mountain escape from the heat, Joshua Tree is the desert-side version, a completely different landscape about the same distance from Indio. Between the two, you've got most of what makes this part of California worth exploring beyond the pool deck.
