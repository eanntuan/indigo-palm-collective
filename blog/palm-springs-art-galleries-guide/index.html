Palm Springs Art Galleries: Uptown to Backstreet Walk

By Eann Tuan · July 11, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick summary: Palm Springs has more serious gallery space per block than a city this size has any right to. Downtown and the Uptown Design District cover the walkable core: Palm Springs Art Museum, Janssen Artspace inside a Donald Wexler building, Stewart Galleries, and the Shag Store. Backstreet Art District, a cluster of artist-owned studios off Cherokee Way, is worth the ten-minute drive, especially on First Wednesday when the artists are in their studios working. October through May is gallery season. Most spaces are small enough to see properly in an afternoon.

I've lived fifteen minutes from downtown Palm Springs for years now, and I still forget how much of it is just... art. Not the "there's a sculpture in the roundabout" kind of city art, though there's that too. Actual working galleries, one after another, in a downtown small enough to walk end to end before you'd need a second coffee.

Most visitors get one museum stop and call it done. That's fine if you have an afternoon. If you have a full day, or you're staying somewhere in the valley for a few nights anyway, the gallery scene here rewards slowing down more than almost anything else in the Coachella Valley.

Forever Marilyn, reinstalled on Museum Way right next to the museum entrance. Free to see any time, no ticket needed.

Downtown: Start at the Museum, Then Walk

The Palm Springs Art Museum is the anchor for a reason. Three floors, a theater, a bistro, and a permanent collection heavy on Western American and Native American art alongside rotating contemporary shows. Thursday nights from 5 to 8pm, admission is free, funded by the city, which is the move if you're trying to keep a trip budget honest.

What people skip is the museum's second building four blocks south: the Architecture and Design Center at 300 S Palm Canyon Drive. It's a former bank, Santa Fe Federal Savings and Loan, built in 1961 by E. Stewart Williams and restored by the same firm that redid the Kaufmann House. The design store now lives inside the original vault, drive-through teller window and all. You go for the building. The art inside is a bonus.

From there it's a five-minute walk to Stewart Galleries at 191-A S Indian Canyon Drive, which has been dealing in Impressionist, Modernist, and Plein Air paintings since 1986 and somehow also holds one of the largest public collections of English and American Majolica pottery anywhere. Not what I expected walking in the first time, and worth ten minutes even if ceramics aren't your thing.

A few blocks east on Tahquitz Canyon Way, Janssen Artspace sits inside the Welmas Building, a late-1960s Donald Wexler design with the glass-and-steel look that defines half the buildings people photograph in this town. Steven Janssen opened the gallery in 2006, closed it during the recession, and reopened in 2017 in this space. The abstract and contemporary work rotates often enough that it's worth a second visit if you're in town more than a few days.

The Wexler look: glass, steel, and a flat roofline. Half the gallery buildings downtown are architecture before they're anything else.

Uptown Design District: Where Palm Canyon Turns Colorful

North Palm Canyon Drive between Alejo Road and Vista Chino is the Uptown Design District, and it's the part of downtown that leans playful instead of formal. The Shag Store at 745 N Palm Canyon Drive is the only retail gallery dedicated to Josh Agle, better known as Shag, whose cocktail-party-in-1962 style is basically shorthand for midcentury Palm Springs at this point. It started as a small storefront in 2010 and has since grown into three thousand square feet of prints, originals, and merchandise.

Two blocks up, the Lofts Art District at 2755 N Palm Canyon Drive houses several smaller galleries under one roof, rotating studios from working local artists. It's less polished than the Shag Store, which is the point. You're seeing work in progress, not just finished product on a wall.

Give yourself an hour minimum for Uptown if you're doing it right. Grab something cold first. It's a lot of sidewalk in the middle of the day.

Beyond Downtown: Where the Working Artists Are

Backstreet Art District, at 2600 S Cherokee Way off Highway 111, is a different animal than downtown. It's ten to twelve artist-owned studios and galleries in an L-shaped building, going since 2005, and most of the people showing work there are also the ones making it in the back room. David A. Clark's cast glass, Gary Wexler's screen prints (yes, Donald Wexler's son, carrying the family name into a different medium), and a rotating cast of painters and ceramicists who set their own hours, which means it's worth checking Instagram before you drive out.

The reason to time your visit is First Wednesday Art Walk, held the first Wednesday of every month from roughly 5 to 7pm. The artists are in, there's usually wine, and you get to talk to the person who made the thing you're looking at. It runs rain or shine, every month, no exceptions for holidays.

Downtown proper has one more stop worth the detour: Imageville at 128 La Plaza, Gary Dorothy's fine art photography gallery since 2002. His large-format prints of the surrounding desert and mountains are the kind of thing that looks better in person than any photo of a photo could show you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time of year to visit Palm Springs art galleries?

October through May. Most galleries keep shorter or unpredictable hours in the summer heat, and several artist studios in Backstreet Art District close or cut back on days entirely from June through September. If you're set on a summer trip, call ahead.

Are Palm Springs art galleries free to visit?

Most galleries are free to walk through, since they're also retail spaces. The Palm Springs Art Museum charges admission (free Thursdays 5 to 8pm), and the Architecture and Design Center is included with museum admission or a separate lower-priced ticket.

How much time should I plan for a Palm Springs gallery day?

Downtown and Uptown together take a comfortable half day on foot. Add Backstreet Art District and you're looking at a full day once you factor in the drive and time spent talking to artists instead of speed-walking past their work.

What is the First Wednesday Art Walk?

A monthly evening event at Backstreet Art District, the first Wednesday of every month from about 5 to 7pm, where the working artist-owners open their studios alongside their galleries. It happens every month regardless of holidays.

Where should I stay for a Palm Springs gallery-focused trip?

The Sundune puts you inside Palm Springs itself, a five-minute drive from both downtown and the Uptown Design District. If you're combining galleries with a broader Coachella Valley trip, Cozy Cactus or Terra Luz in Indio are about 25 minutes out and work well as a home base.

I still haven't made it to every gallery on this list in one visit, and I've had years to try. That's kind of the point. Palm Springs rewards the slow version of a trip more than the checklist version. If you're planning the rest of your stay around midcentury architecture too, the Palm Springs midcentury architecture walk pairs naturally with a gallery day, and if you're building a trip around Modernism Week specifically, most of these galleries run extended hours and special shows that week.
