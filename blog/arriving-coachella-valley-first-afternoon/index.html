Arriving in the Coachella Valley: Your First Afternoon

By Eann Tuan · June 22, 2026 · 5 min read

You made it. The car is cooling down in the driveway and the house smells faintly of sunscreen from whoever was here before you, which is a good sign. You have a whole trip ahead and approximately seventeen ideas about what to do first.

Here's the thing: the first afternoon almost always goes better when you slow down and do less than you planned. The Coachella Valley rewards that instinct more than most places do. This is a loose guide to landing well, whether you're staying in Indio, Palm Springs, or somewhere in between.

Groceries Before Anything Else

Don't skip this. The single best thing you can do in the first hour is stock the kitchen so tomorrow morning isn't a scramble. Tired guests who skip the grocery run end up paying $28 for breakfast at a resort restaurant. That's fine once. It's not fine every day.

If you're staying in Indio (near the Terra Luz or Cozy Cactus side of the valley), Stater Bros on Monroe Street is your fastest option, about eight minutes from the Indian Palms neighborhood. It covers everything. For a better wine selection or actual produce, Whole Foods in Palm Desert is worth the extra twenty minutes, especially if you're stocking up for a longer stay.

If you're in Palm Springs, Trader Joe's on East Palm Canyon Drive is the move for most things. Ralphs is nearby and open late if you forget something at 10pm.

Buy more than you think you need. You will eat at the house more than you planned.

A stocked kitchen makes every morning better. Spend the twenty minutes.

The Pool: Timing Matters More Than You Think

Every first-time desert guest wants to jump in the pool the moment they arrive. In winter and spring, go ahead. In summer, the answer is more complicated.

The water will be warm. The sun from 11am to 4pm will be brutal. Getting in is fine; the getting out part, standing on a deck that's been absorbing heat for six hours, is not. The sweet spot is 5:30pm onward, once the sun drops behind the fence line and the air starts to shift. By 7 or 8pm, the pool is genuinely perfect and you'll end up staying out until you didn't mean to.

If you arrive in the afternoon during summer, unpack, get the groceries, and wait. The pool isn't going anywhere.

The outdoor hours in the Coachella Valley belong to early morning and evening. Everything in between is optional.

Tonight's Dinner: One Honest Recommendation

You're probably not cooking on the first night. That's fine.

If you're in Indio, our local guide covers the neighborhood in detail, but the short answer for a first-night dinner is Habanero Mexican Grill on Highway 111. It's unpretentious, the margaritas are good, and the portions are serious. You'll be home before 9pm and that's probably the right call after a travel day.

If you're in Palm Springs, Sherman's Deli & Bakery on North Indian Canyon has been feeding people since 1963 and runs on the same logic: it's a lot of food for a fair price, and there's no reason to overthink dinner when you just got here.

Both of these are the kind of place you'd go back to on a second trip. Save the more considered restaurant for night two, when you have your bearings.

What to Skip on Day One

Joshua Tree will be there tomorrow, and it's better in the morning anyway before the heat builds. The outlet mall in Desert Hills will not be more interesting after a four-hour drive. The impulse to "maximize" the first afternoon is the thing most likely to make the trip feel rushed.

Day one is for landing softly. A walk around the neighborhood after dinner is enough. The trip improves when you stop treating the first afternoon like a race to see everything.

The backyard has been sitting here waiting for you. It'll still be here after dinner.

A Note on the Heat

If this is your first summer trip to the Coachella Valley, the heat will still surprise you even if you read about it beforehand. A few practical things: keep the house closed from about noon to 4pm and let the A/C do its job. Drink water before you feel thirsty, especially if you're drinking alcohol. The difference between a day that feels great and a day that feels terrible is usually just hydration.

The nights are legitimately beautiful. Warm, dry, and quiet in a way that's hard to find elsewhere. Plan to be outside after dark.

The best hours are at the edges of the day. The valley rewards people who stay up a little later than they planned.

Planning the Rest of the Trip

Once you're unpacked and fed, the valley opens up quickly. If you're in Indio, the full guide to things to do in Indio and the surrounding area is a good next read before bed. If you're splitting time between Indio and Palm Springs, the downtown strip, the Indian Canyons, and the Aerial Tram are all worth a half-day each.

The best trips here tend to mix a few destination activities with a lot of time at the house. The house is part of the experience, not just where you sleep. You probably figured that out the moment you walked in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do first when arriving at a Coachella Valley vacation rental?

Get groceries before you do anything else. Stater Bros in Indio and Trader Joe's in Palm Springs are the most convenient options. A stocked house makes every subsequent day easier.

What grocery stores are near vacation rentals in Indio, CA?

Stater Bros on Monroe Street is the closest option for most Indio rentals, about 8 minutes from the Indian Palms neighborhood. Whole Foods in Palm Desert is 20-25 minutes away for better produce and wine.

What time of day is best to use the pool in the Coachella Valley during summer?

Evening is the right answer, specifically from about 5:30pm onward. The deck cools down, the sun drops, and the water has had all day to warm up. The midday pool is usable but the experience is much better after 5pm.

How hot does it get in the Coachella Valley in summer?

Temperatures regularly reach 110-115°F in July and August. Keep the house closed midday, stay hydrated, and plan outdoor activities for early morning or evening. The heat is manageable with the right habits and significantly less manageable without them.

Is there anything to do near vacation rentals in Indio besides Coachella?

Yes, and a lot of it is underrated. The date farms along Shields Drive, Joshua Tree National Park about 45 minutes north, the Salton Sea for a genuinely strange afternoon, and the Coachella Valley's restaurant scene are all worth building into the trip. See our full Indio area guide for specifics.
