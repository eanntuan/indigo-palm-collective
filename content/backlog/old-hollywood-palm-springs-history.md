Old Hollywood came to Palm Springs because studio contracts kept stars within roughly two hours of the lot, and the desert was about as far as that leash reached. Between the 1930s and the 1960s that two-hour radius filled the town with tennis clubs, hideaway hotels, and modern houses, and a surprising amount of it is still standing.

## How Palm Springs Became Hollywood's Desert Retreat

The line you will see most often is that stars were bound to stay within a set distance of the studio. Sources that describe it, including <a href="https://visitpalmsprings.com/blog/post/palm-springs-hollywood-history/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Visit Greater Palm Springs</strong></a>, frame it as a two-hour radius in case a performer was called to set, and I could not find the contract wording itself, so it is best read as well-worn local lore.

The town already had a resort reputation by 1927, when the Spanish Revival <strong>El Mirador Hotel</strong> opened with a bell tower, an Olympic-sized pool, and tennis courts. Visit Greater Palm Springs lists guests such as Shirley Temple, Errol Flynn, Lucille Ball, and Bing Crosby. The hotel became a military hospital in World War II, the site is now Desert Regional Medical Center, and a replica of the tower stands there today.

## The Racquet Club and the Rules of the Desert

The better origin story belongs to two actors, Charlie Farrell and Ralph Bellamy, who started the <strong>Racquet Club</strong> in 1934. According to <a href="https://theclio.com/entry/65387" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Clio</a>, the courts were dedicated that Christmas Day, play cost $1, and the first day's take was under $20. A pool, the Bamboo Room bar, and bungalows came next, and regulars through the years included Clark Gable, Spencer Tracy, Ginger Rogers, Judy Garland, and Frank Sinatra.

<img src="/blog/images/old-hollywood-palm-springs-1.webp" alt="Black-and-white 1953 newspaper photo of tennis player Nancy Chaffee dancing with her husband, baseball player Ralph Kiner, at the Racquet Club of Palm Springs" width="1004" height="1639" loading="lazy" style="max-width:420px;height:auto;display:block;margin:0 auto">
<p class="image-caption">Nancy Chaffee and Ralph Kiner at the Racquet Club in 1953. Photo: unknown author / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nancy_Chaffee_and_Ralph_Kiner_1953.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wikimedia Commons</a>, public domain.</p>

Farrell later served as mayor, from 1948 to 1953, which shows how tightly the resort and the town were wound together. Another local habit shaped the era as well: leave the stars alone. Bob Hope golfed in peace and Sinatra could stroll downtown with an ice cream cone, and that unwritten rule is part of why they kept coming back.

Marilyn Monroe belongs in this chapter too. As Visit Greater Palm Springs tells it, photographer Bruno Bernard spotted her, then still Norma Jeane Baker, during a poolside shoot at the Racquet Club in 1947, which led to an introduction to agent Johnny Hyde. Clio's entry on the club includes a 1949 photo of her on the diving board.

## Palm Springs Celebrity Homes: Sinatra, Hope, and Elvis

Sinatra is the headline act. He commissioned architect E. Stewart Williams to design <strong>Twin Palms</strong> in 1947, a Desert Modern house with a piano-shaped pool, and the house later hosted his marriages to Nancy Barbato and Ava Gardner, according to <a href="https://www.visitcalifornia.com/experience/twin-palms/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Visit California</a>. Decades later he held a dinner at Melvyn's the Saturday before his July 1976 wedding to Barbara Marx.

Bob and Dolores Hope commissioned architect John Lautner in 1969 to design a house on the highest building site in Palm Springs, according to <a href="https://www.arch.columbia.edu/events/1514-restoring-hope-the-restoration-of-the-bob-dolores-hope-house-in-palm-springs-ca" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Columbia GSAPP</a>. It is a private home, so the view from a distance is the one on offer.

Then there is the Elvis Honeymoon Hideaway, where Elvis and Priscilla Presley spent time after their May 1967 wedding. Tours of the house have run on and off over the years and its status has changed with ownership, so check with the house before you plan around going inside. From the street it is a curved midcentury house on a quiet bend, and the usual rule applies: stay on the public side of the curb.

## Palm Springs Celebrity Homes Tour Options

The most reliable guided option is the <a href="https://pshistoricalsociety.org/collections/tours" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Palm Springs Historical Society</strong></a>, whose walking tours have included "Stars of the Movie Colony" and "Frank Sinatra's Neighborhood." They are guided and exterior only, and the Society's tour page has the current schedule, ages, and pricing, which change by season. The Society also runs the McCallum Adobe and Cornelia White House museums on South Palm Canyon Drive, so call or check its site for current hours.

## Old Hollywood Hotels in Palm Springs

The history is easiest to sleep inside, and several of these places started as private estates or guest houses.

<img src="/blog/images/old-hollywood-palm-springs-5.webp" alt="Ingleside Inn in Palm Springs, a Spanish Colonial Revival building with stucco walls and a tile roof among palm trees" width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">Ingleside Inn in 2014. Photo: Visitor7 / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ingleside_Inn_01.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wikimedia Commons</a>, CC BY-SA 3.0.</p>

<a href="https://inglesideestate.com/history/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Ingleside Estate</strong></a> began in 1922 as a Spanish Colonial Revival estate designed by George Washington Smith for the Pierce-Arrow heirs. Ruth Hardy turned it into an invitation-only club in the late 1930s (sources give several dates), and the hotel says Marilyn Monroe, Elizabeth Taylor, and Marlon Brando signed its register. Mel Haber bought it in 1975 and opened Melvyn's, which still operates on West Ramon Road.

<strong>The Willows Historic Palm Springs Inn</strong> was built in the 1920s as a winter home for William and Nella Mead and later passed to the attorney Samuel Untermyer, whose guests included Albert Einstein. <strong>Casa Cody</strong>, built in 1937 by Harriet Cody, is billed by Visit Greater Palm Springs as the oldest continuously operating hotel in town. <strong>Korakia Pensione</strong> dates to 1924, and its reported guests include Rudolph Valentino and Errol Flynn.

For a drink instead of a room, <a href="https://visitpalmsprings.com/blog/post/frank-sinatra-palm-springs-hangouts-self-guided-tour/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Bootlegger Tiki</strong></a> now occupies the old Don the Beachcomber space, where Sinatra hosted dinners in 1954, and the Purple Room has been around since September 1960. Our <a href="/blog/palm-springs-3-day-itinerary/">3 day Palm Springs itinerary</a> shows how to fit those stops into a longer trip.

## What Is Gone From Old Palm Springs

The Racquet Club is the sad one. Farrell sold it in 1959, it opened to the public in 1986, a late-1990s revival closed after about four years, and a 2014 fire destroyed many of the remaining buildings, according to Clio. Treat the site as closed. El Mirador, the Doll House, and Ruby's Dunes are demolished too.

## FAQ

### Why did old Hollywood stars go to Palm Springs?
Studio contracts kept stars within about two hours of the lot in case they were called to set, and Palm Springs was close enough to qualify but far enough to feel private. The town also had a leave-the-celebrities-alone culture.

### Can you tour Frank Sinatra's house in Palm Springs?
Twin Palms is a private home that has mostly opened to the public through Modernism Week tours, and it is also rented for stays and events. The Historical Society's Sinatra neighborhood walking tour shows the area from the street.

### What is the best Palm Springs celebrity homes tour?
For history, start with the Palm Springs Historical Society walking tours, which are guided and exterior only. Open-air cart tours cover more ground in less time, and a free self-guided driving map from Visit Greater Palm Springs works too. Check each operator's current schedule and prices before booking.

### Which old Hollywood hotels in Palm Springs are still open?
Ingleside Estate, The Willows Historic Palm Springs Inn, Casa Cody, and Korakia Pensione are all operating hotels with roots in the old Hollywood era. El Mirador Hotel and the Racquet Club are gone.

### Can I visit the Racquet Club of Palm Springs today?
No. The club closed years ago and a 2014 fire destroyed many of its remaining buildings, so the site is not open to visitors.
