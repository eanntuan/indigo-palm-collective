<p><strong>Quick summary:</strong> The best Palm Springs murals and Coachella Valley street art sit in four clusters: Coachella's Pueblo Viejo, Old Town Indio, Palm Desert, and downtown Desert Hot Springs, with a few single walls in Palm Springs itself. Drive east to west or the reverse, park once per cluster, and walk the rest. Everything here is free. Walls get repainted, so use the official city lists linked below for the current map.</p>

<p>The question most people type is where to find murals in the Coachella Valley, and the short answer is Coachella first. The farmworker portrait at 6th Street and Vine Avenue, painted by LA artist El Mac, anchors a downtown program called Coachella Walls that began in 2014. From there the route runs through Indio, Palm Desert, and Desert Hot Springs, with Palm Springs as the polished finale.</p>

<p>I own two rental houses in Indio, so this guide starts at the east end of the valley, where the walls are older and the parking is easier.</p>

<h2>Coachella Murals: Pueblo Viejo and Coachella Walls</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.visitgreaterpalmsprings.com/blog/post/your-guide-to-public-art-in-greater-palm-springs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Visit Greater Palm Springs public art guide</strong></a> lists El Mac's <em>Anonymous Farm Worker</em> at 6th Street and Vine Avenue in the Pueblo Viejo neighborhood. Around the corner, Sego y Obval's <em>La Cosecha del Futuro</em> is at 722 Vine Avenue, and Ricardo Angeles Mendoza's <em>Hungry Dog of the Desert</em> is at Veterans Memorial Park on the 1500 block of 4th Street.</p>

<p>Edoardo Tresoldi's <em>Etherea</em>, a 54-foot wire-mesh sculpture, stands on 6th Street downtown with its own small park. The City of Coachella runs an <a href="https://www.coachella.org/residents/art-in-public-places" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Art in Public Places</strong></a> page and a walking tour brochure that cover the rest of the walls, including more on 6th Street.</p>

<p>Park on 6th Street or a side street and walk. The blocks are short, and the walls are close enough that the whole cluster is a loop on foot.</p>

<h2>Indio Murals: Old Town and a Newer Wall</h2>

<p>Indio started a historic mural project in 1996, and the older walls cluster around Old Town. Bijan Masoumpanah's <em>Old Indio</em> is at Miles Avenue and Towne Street, Nicole Ponsler's <em>Rebirth</em> is at 82-660 Miles Avenue, and David Ocelotl Garcia's <em>Moving Towards the Future</em> is at 45125 Smurr Street, all per the same Visit Greater Palm Springs guide.</p>

<img src="/blog/images/indio-sign-miles-ave.webp" alt="Rainbow-colored INDIO letter sign in downtown Indio with palm trees and string lights" width="1400" height="933" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">The INDIO letters are the easy photo stop downtown, a short drive from the mural addresses on Miles Avenue.</p>

<p>The newest wall is <em>Nuestra Vida</em> by Indio native Adam Enrique Rodriguez, at Avenue 45 and Jackson Street. <a href="https://kesq.com/news/2026/02/01/indio-native-unveils-first-large-scale-public-mural-celebrating-citys-history/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>KESQ</strong></a> reported he was chosen from roughly 30 applicants, started painting in November 2025, and had a ribbon-cutting set for early February 2026. The design moves from day to night and draws on the Date Festival and the Coachella festival.</p>

<p>The Indio City Council also selected Palm Desert artist John Cuevas in March 2025 for a mural at Indio Center Stage, per <a href="https://riversiderecord.org/indio-council-selects-artist-john-cuevas-of-palm-desert-for-center-stage-mural-project/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>The Riverside Record</strong></a>. The approved design shows fan palms, desert tortoises, and a set of butterfly wings meant for photos. Check the city's public art page for whether it is finished before you plan a stop around it.</p>

<p>If you are staying in town, the <a href="/blog/indio-local-gems/">Indio local gems guide</a> has dates, tamales, and coffee for the stretch between walls.</p>

<h2>Palm Desert Murals: John Cuevas and El Paseo</h2>

<p>Cuevas started his <em>Unite Palm Desert</em> murals in 2020 as part of the city's COVID relief program. <a href="https://www.dunnedwards.com/pros/blog/how-one-mystical-mural-is-uniting-strangers-and-friends-alike-in-palm-desert/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Crystal Field</strong></a> sits at Highway 111 and Cabrillo Avenue on the old Valley Lumber Company building. The mural is 65 by 15 feet, built on an equilateral triangle with the word "unite" shaped like a heart at the center, in ochres pulled from the building's yellow brick.</p>

<p>The building itself was designed by modernist architect Walter S. White in the mid 1950s, which makes the stop a two-for-one if you like architecture. Our <a href="/blog/palm-springs-midcentury-architecture-tour/">midcentury architecture tour</a> covers the Palm Springs side of that story.</p>

<p>On El Paseo, Lindsey Made This's <em>We Hope in Color</em> is at 73545 El Paseo, inside The Gardens on El Paseo. Parking in the Gardens structure beats hunting for street spots along El Paseo, and the <a href="/blog/things-to-do-palm-desert/">Palm Desert guide</a> has lunch options within a few minutes.</p>

<h2>Desert Hot Springs Murals: 13 Walls in One Walkable Downtown</h2>

<p>This is the densest stretch on the route. The city's <a href="https://www.cityofdhs.org/experience-art/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>public art page</strong></a> lists 13 murals around Pierson Boulevard and Palm Drive, tied to the Known Gallery curated program, with a street address for each. Shepard Fairey's is at 66925 Pierson Boulevard, Benjamin Jeanjean's at 12525 Palm Drive, and Cryptik's at 12070 Palm Drive.</p>

<p>Other names on the list include Push, Jaque Fragua, Victor Reyes, Royyal Dog, DabsMyla, Yanoe, Mr. B Baby, Euphoric, Trav Msk, and Weirdocult. Some outlets give a Palm Drive address for the Fairey piece, so follow the city's list in your map app and trust the walls you can see.</p>

<p>Park once on Pierson and walk the grid. It is flat and open with little shade, so go early from October through April and bring water.</p>

<h2>Palm Springs Street Art: Fewer Walls, Plenty of Public Art</h2>

<p>Palm Springs has fewer giant murals than Coachella or Desert Hot Springs, though the <a href="https://pspublicarts.com/gerald-clarke-road-signs/past-projects-murals/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Palm Springs Public Arts</strong></a> program keeps a list of past mural projects, and the city's art lists include <em>Woman in Glasses</em> by James Haunt and Zes MSK at 2481 North Palm Canyon Drive in the Uptown Design District.</p>

<h2>Desert X and Other Public Art</h2>

<p><a href="https://desertx.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Desert X</strong></a> is the biennial that put the valley on the land art map. It is free and open to all, and its next edition is scheduled for October 30, 2027 through May 7, 2028, per the organizers' announcement. It is a land art show, so it adds sculpture and installation to a mural trip instead of more walls.</p>

<h2>Best Light, Parking, and a Half-Day Route</h2>

<img src="/blog/images/coachella-valley-murals-3.webp" alt="Empty Palm Canyon Drive in downtown Palm Springs early in the morning with tall palms and a clear sky" width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">Downtown Palm Springs at first light. Photo: Another Believer / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Palm_Springs,_CA,_2024_-_028.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wikimedia Commons</a>, CC BY-SA 4.0.</p>

<p>Light is the cheapest upgrade to any mural photo. Check which way a wall faces on your map's satellite view, and shoot it when the sun is on it rather than behind it. In summer, early morning or the last hour before sunset keeps colors from washing out, and Desert Hot Springs gets hot fast.</p>

<p>Parking is usually free street parking in Coachella, Indio, and Desert Hot Springs, and a garage or lot in Palm Desert and downtown Palm Springs. Arrive earlier on weekends, especially on festival weekends when Indio fills up.</p>

<p>For a half-day route from Indio, start in Coachella's Pueblo Viejo, then move to Old Town Indio for the older murals and Avenue 45, then finish in Palm Desert at Crystal Field and The Gardens on El Paseo. From Palm Springs, start with the Uptown wall, walk downtown, then drive north for the Desert Hot Springs grid if you have energy left.</p>

<h2>Where to Stay Near the Murals</h2>

If you would rather be near Uptown and the Palm Springs stops, <a href="/the-sundune/">The Sundune</a> is in Palm Springs.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Where are the best murals in the Coachella Valley?</h3>
<p>Coachella's Pueblo Viejo district and downtown Desert Hot Springs have the densest clusters of large murals. Old Town Indio and Palm Desert add city-commissioned work, including John Cuevas's Crystal Field.</p>

<h3>Are the Palm Springs murals free to visit?</h3>
<p>Yes, every mural named here is on a public wall or in a public courtyard. The only cost is parking, and most spots use free street parking.</p>

<h3>What is the best time of day to photograph the murals?</h3>
<p>Whenever the sun is on the wall, so check which way it faces. Early morning also beats midday heat from May through September.</p>

<h3>Do I need a car for a self-guided mural tour?</h3>
<p>For the full valley route, yes. The clusters are miles apart, but inside Coachella's Pueblo Viejo and downtown Desert Hot Springs you can park once and walk.</p>

<h3>How do I know a mural is still there?</h3>
<p>Check the <a href="https://www.cityofdhs.org/experience-art/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Desert Hot Springs</a> and <a href="https://www.coachella.org/residents/art-in-public-places" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Coachella</a> public art pages or the artist's page before you go. Walls get repainted, and the official lists stay more current than any blog post.</p>
