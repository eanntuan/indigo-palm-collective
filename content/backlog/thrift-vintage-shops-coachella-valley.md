<p>For thrift stores in Palm Springs, start with the charity shops Revivals and Angel View, then add the Palm Springs Vintage Market on the first Sunday of the month. For midcentury furniture, Re[x] and Boomerang for Modern on North Palm Canyon Drive are the serious stops, and Palm Desert's El Paseo is where designer consignment lives.</p>

<p>The valley has an odd secondhand economy. Retirees downsize out of midcentury houses, snowbirds redecorate every few winters, and good furniture ends up on showroom floors and charity racks. Shops change hours often, so every stop below links to its own site or a tourism-bureau listing. Check before you drive.</p>

<h2>Vintage Shops in Palm Springs for Midcentury Furniture</h2>

<img src="/blog/images/ps-midcentury-teal-door.webp" alt="Teal front door on a midcentury modern home in Palm Springs" width="1200" height="1800" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">Palm Springs has thousands of midcentury homes, which is why the vintage furniture trade here runs so deep. More on the houses in our <a href="/blog/palm-springs-midcentury-architecture/">midcentury architecture guide</a>.</p>

<h3>Re[x]</h3>
<p><a href="https://rex.haus/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Re[x]</strong></a> has been in Palm Springs since 1998 and describes its showroom as 11,000 square feet, at 2500 North Palm Canyon Drive, Suite B1. The stock runs sofas, dining tables, lighting, rugs, beds and art books, a mix of vintage and contemporary midcentury pieces.</p>

<p>It also ships, which matters when you fall for a sofa and flew in. Small items go by UPS or USPS, and big furniture travels freight through uShip with curbside, threshold or white-glove delivery. The shop's own estimate for freight is roughly 7 to 21 business days, and it recommends white-glove for large or fragile pieces.</p>

<h3>Boomerang for Modern</h3>
<p><a href="https://www.boomerangformodern.com/about.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Boomerang for Modern</strong></a> has sold vintage modern furniture since 1985, much of it from local estates, along with some new pieces from the original makers. The showroom is about 1,100 square feet on North Palm Canyon Drive, a Herman Miller retailer, and the shop says it is glad to arrange shipping.</p>

<p>It is smaller than Re[x], so the edit is tighter. The shop says some items can be rented and that it takes occasional photo styling projects, which is useful if you are staging a rental, a topic our <a href="/blog/staging-vacation-rental-photo-shoot/">staging guide</a> covers from the host side.</p>

<h3>The Uptown Design District</h3>
<p>Both shops sit near the Uptown Design District, the stretch of North Palm Canyon Drive where Palm Springs keeps its vintage and design stores. The <a href="https://visitpalmsprings.com/blog/post/palm-springs-uptown-vintage-shopping/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Visit Palm Springs vintage guide</a> lists the neighbors, from antiques and Atomic Age glassware to patio furniture and vintage clothing. It also covers The Shops at Thirteen Forty-Five, a complex of design shops in a 1955 building by architect E. Stewart Williams.</p>

<p>Plan two hours for the strip, longer if you read every price tag. Our <a href="/blog/palm-springs-neighborhood-guide/">neighborhood guide</a> explains where Uptown sits relative to downtown.</p>

<h2>Palm Springs Vintage Market and Charity Thrift</h2>

<img src="/blog/images/thrift-vintage-coachella-valley-1.webp" alt="Shoppers walking between vendor tents and tables at the Vintage Market in Palm Springs under palm trees" width="1600" height="1067" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">Photo: Visitor7 / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vintage_Market_(Palm_Springs)_01.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0</a></p>

<h3>Palm Springs Vintage Market</h3>
<p>The <a href="https://palmspringsvintagemarket.com/know-before-you-go" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Palm Springs Vintage Market</strong></a> runs the first Sunday of every month. From fall through spring it sits outdoors at 356 N Calle Encilia, between Amado and Alejo, and in summer it moves indoors to 403 S Pavilion Way. Early entry runs 7 to 8am for $10, and general admission is $5 from 8am to 2pm.</p>

<img src="/blog/images/thrift-vintage-coachella-valley-2.webp" alt="Vintage clothing, a mannequin and a chartreuse sofa at a vendor booth with mountains behind at the Palm Springs Vintage Market" width="1600" height="1280" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">Photo: Visitor7 / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vintage_Market_(Palm_Springs)_02.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0</a></p>

<p>Parking is free in several lots on each side, and leashed, well-trained dogs are allowed. The market's own advice is to expect a full day of sun, so bring a hat. Pay the $10 if you want first pick of lamps and small furniture.</p>

<h3>Revivals and Angel View</h3>
<p><a href="https://revivalsstores.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Revivals</strong></a> is the Desert AIDS Project, now DAP Health, thrift chain, with stores in Palm Springs, Cathedral City and Palm Desert. <a href="https://angelview.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>Angel View</strong></a> is a local nonprofit serving children and adults with disabilities, and its resale stores fund that work. Angel View's site lists store hours of 10am to 6pm Monday through Saturday and 10am to 5pm Sunday. Revivals posts different hours on different pages, so call the store you plan to visit.</p>

<img src="/blog/images/thrift-vintage-coachella-valley-4.webp" alt="Racks of clothing and a shelf of handbags inside a secondhand shop" width="1600" height="1200" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">A typical secondhand rack, photographed in Prague. Photo: MAKY.OREL / <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Clothing_racks_in_second_hand_shop,_Prague.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0</a></p>

<p>Charity stores mix vintage in with everything else, so the skill is scanning fast. Go early in the day, check the furniture floor first, and look for solid wood over veneer.</p>

<p>A few habits help in any of them. Open every drawer, because water stains and sticky runners show up there first. Turn lamps over to check the cord and the maker's mark, and sit on any chair before you carry it to the register. Small pieces such as ceramics, barware and framed prints are the easiest to fly home, so if you are traveling light, shop that end of the floor.</p>

<h2>How to Shop Vintage and Thrift Stores in the Coachella Valley</h2>

<h3>Go Thursday through Sunday</h3>
<p>Many independent shops here keep short hours early in the week, and some close Tuesday and Wednesday. Weekends are busier, and the Vintage Market only runs on a Sunday.</p>

<p>Modernism Week in February is the busiest stretch for design-minded visitors, so shopping outside it likely means a calmer showroom and more time to talk to the staff.</p>

<h3>Estate sales (general advice)</h3>
<p>Estate sale listings on <a href="https://www.estatesales.net/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">EstateSales.net</a> can be filtered by city. In general, sales open on the first day with the best selection and the discounts arrive on the last, so choose based on how badly you want a specific piece. Bring cash, a tape measure and a friend who can lift.</p>

<h3>Check for desert damage</h3>
<p>Sun and heat fade finishes and break down cushion foam and vinyl, so inspect surfaces before buying. The <a href="/blog/outdoor-furniture-desert-heat/">outdoor furniture guide</a> goes deeper on what holds up in 115-degree summers.</p>

<img src="/blog/images/cc-living-room-v2.webp" alt="Living room at The Cozy Cactus in Indio with a sectional sofa, foosball table and wall art" width="1800" height="1201" loading="lazy">
<p class="image-caption">The living room at The Cozy Cactus in Indio, a room with space for one good secondhand chair or credenza.</p>

<h3>Measure first, then plan the ride home</h3>
<p>Write down your cargo dimensions before you leave and measure the doorways at home. Pack a moving blanket and ratchet straps, since shops will not always lend them. If a piece will not fit, ask about freight and photograph it before it ships.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What are the best thrift stores in Palm Springs?</h3>
<p>Revivals and Angel View are the two charity chains with Palm Springs stores, and the Palm Springs Vintage Market covers the first Sunday of each month. For midcentury furniture, Re[x] and Boomerang for Modern are stronger than either charity store.</p>

<h3>Where can I find midcentury modern furniture in Palm Springs?</h3>
<p>Re[x] has an 11,000 square foot showroom of vintage and contemporary midcentury pieces on North Palm Canyon Drive. Boomerang for Modern, also on North Palm Canyon, has sold vintage modern furniture from local estates since 1985.</p>

<h3>Is there good consignment in Palm Desert?</h3>
<p>The Private Collection at 73925 El Paseo sells furnishings, lighting, rugs and art on consignment, open Tuesday through Saturday from 9 to 5 according to its site. Confirm hours before you drive over.</p>

<h3>Can I ship furniture home from a Palm Springs vintage shop?</h3>
<p>Often yes. Re[x] ships small items by UPS or USPS and large furniture by freight through uShip, and Boomerang for Modern says it will arrange shipping. Ask for a quote before you buy.</p>

<h3>When is the Palm Springs Vintage Market?</h3>
<p>The first Sunday of every month, 7am to 2pm. From fall through spring it is outdoors at 356 N Calle Encilia, with early entry from 7 to 8am for $10 and $5 general admission after that.</p>
