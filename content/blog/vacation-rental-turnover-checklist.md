<p>Every 4-star review that mentions cleanliness traces back to one missed thing, not a bad clean overall. It's almost never "the whole house was dirty." It's hair on a pillow, coffee grounds in the machine, or a partial roll of toilet paper waiting on the holder. The checklist exists because the same thing should never get missed twice.</p>

<p>We run three properties in the Coachella Valley: <a href="/terra-luz/">Terra Luz</a> (3BR/2BA in Indio with a Kahlo blue pool), The Cozy Cactus (3BR/2BA in Indio at Indian Palms Country Club), and The Sundune (2BR/2BA in Palm Springs, coastal-desert feel). Combined, that's 191 reviews and a lot of refinement. What follows is the actual checklist we hand off before every turnover, organized room by room. Take what works, adjust the specifics to your property, and give your cleaner something concrete to follow.</p>

<img src="/blog/images/terra-luz-primary-bedroom-wide.webp" alt="Terra Luz vacation rental primary bedroom in Indio California staged between guests" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="533" height="800">

<h2>Entry and Living Room</h2>

<p>First impressions happen before guests even sit down. The living room sets the tone for everything that follows, so it needs to feel intentional, not just clean.</p>

<ul>
  <li>All throw pillows in place and symmetrical</li>
  <li>Remotes grouped together in one spot, not scattered across cushions</li>
  <li>No fingerprints on sliding glass doors or windows (guests walk up to these immediately)</li>
  <li>Charging cables untangled, visible, and accessible</li>
  <li>Welcome book open to the first page, not buried under a stack of things</li>
  <li>All lights working: a 60-second bulb check prevents a complaint that reads worse than it sounds in a review</li>
</ul>

<h2>Kitchen</h2>

<p>The kitchen is where most cleanliness complaints originate. Guests cook, open cabinets, look inside the microwave. They notice what they're touching.</p>

<ul>
  <li>All dishes washed, dried, and back in the correct cabinets, not just stacked in the drying rack</li>
  <li>Coffee maker emptied and rinsed: leftover grounds are the single fastest path to a complaint</li>
  <li>Counters cleared of any items the previous guests left behind</li>
  <li>Inside of the microwave wiped down (people look in there before they use it)</li>
  <li>Fridge checked: remove any food from the previous stay, wipe shelves and the interior door</li>
  <li>Dish soap topped up, sponge replaced</li>
  <li>Paper towels replaced, not just "enough left on the roll"</li>
</ul>

<img src="/blog/images/terra-luz-kitchen.webp" alt="Terra Luz vacation rental kitchen in Indio California, clean and ready for guests" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="800" height="533">

<h2>Bedrooms</h2>

<p>Run this for each bedroom individually. The temptation is to treat secondary bedrooms as lower priority, but guests staying in them don't feel that way.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Fresh linens with no hair on the pillows: pull the pillowcases fully off and shake them before putting new ones on</li>
  <li>Beds made to hotel standard with corners tucked and no wrinkles on the duvet</li>
  <li>Nightstand surfaces cleared and wiped</li>
  <li>All drawers and closets checked for items left behind by previous guests</li>
  <li>Blinds or curtains positioned consistently, not left wherever the last guest put them</li>
</ul>

<p>The hair issue comes up more than anything else in our feedback. Shaking pillowcases takes ten extra seconds and removes the most common complaint in vacation rental reviews. We learned this the hard way before it showed up in our <a href="/blog/airbnb-hosting-lessons/">first year of hosting lessons</a>.</p>

<h2>Bathrooms</h2>

<p>Bathrooms get scrutinized harder than anywhere else. Guests are barefoot and up close. Nothing hides here.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Toilet scrubbed including under the rim</li>
  <li>Mirror wiped streak-free</li>
  <li>All surfaces cleaned: sink, counter, shower ledge, floor</li>
  <li>New toilet paper roll on the holder on arrival day, never a partial one</li>
  <li>Backup rolls visible and stocked (we keep two under the sink)</li>
  <li>Towels fresh and folded, placed the same way every turnover</li>
  <li>Shampoo, conditioner, and body wash levels checked and topped up or replaced</li>
</ul>

<p>The partial toilet paper roll on arrival is a small thing that reads as neglect. Guests don't think "the previous guest used most of it," they think "no one checked." A fresh roll costs almost nothing and removes that read entirely.</p>

<h2>Outdoor and Pool Area</h2>

<p>At Terra Luz and Cozy Cactus, both walking distance to the Empire Polo Club, the outdoor space is a major draw. Guests spend real time outside, and the pool gets used hard. At The Sundune, the patio and outdoor furniture matter just as much even without a pool.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Pool surface skimmed, filter checked</li>
  <li>Outdoor furniture wiped down: birds, dust, and sunscreen residue accumulate fast in the desert heat</li>
  <li>Towel hooks cleared of any towels from the previous stay</li>
  <li>Outdoor kitchen surfaces wiped</li>
  <li>String lights tested</li>
  <li>All trash from the previous stay removed, including around the pool deck</li>
</ul>

<h2>The Final Walkthrough</h2>

<p>This happens as the last person out. Not before the cleaner finishes, after. One person does this as a dedicated pass, not as a side task.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Set thermostat to arrival temperature</li>
  <li>Confirm the front door locks behind you</li>
  <li>Check that all windows are closed</li>
  <li>Take a quick photo of the property in staging condition: if a dispute comes up later, that photo matters more than anything you could say</li>
</ul>

<p>The welcome book deserves its own mention here: it should be open to the first page, placed somewhere visible, not tucked in a drawer. We cover how we set ours up in our <a href="/blog/vacation-rental-welcome-book/">vacation rental welcome book guide</a>, but the short version is that a guest should be able to find the wifi password and the pool heater instructions without asking.</p>

<p>And the staging photo: it does double duty. It's useful for disputes, but it also holds you accountable to a standard. If you're embarrassed to look at it, something wasn't done right. We started doing this after our first few months and it's one of the habits that fed directly into our review scores. More on how we stage for photography in our <a href="/blog/staging-vacation-rental-photo-shoot/">staging and photo shoot post</a>.</p>

<h2>The Thing Guests Notice More Than Anything Else: Smell</h2>

<p>A property can be visually spotless and still get a cleanliness complaint if the air feels wrong. Musty, stale, or heavy with air freshener all read the same way to guests: something is being covered up.</p>

<p>We run the AC for at least 30 minutes before the turnover is complete, which clears out any trapped air from a closed property. We use a light, unscented linen spray in the bedrooms, nothing floral or synthetic. Heavy plug-in air fresheners are off the list entirely because guests who are sensitive to fragrance will mention it, and guests who aren't sensitive still associate the smell with masking rather than clean.</p>

<p>Good ventilation and clean surfaces handle 90% of this. The spray is just a finishing layer, not a substitute.</p>

<h2>Why Consistency Matters More Than Perfection</h2>

<p>The checklist does something beyond catching missed items: it creates a consistent experience. Guests who return to one of our properties find the throw pillows in the same position, the welcome book open in the same spot, the towels folded the same way. That repetition reads as care, even subconsciously. It's the difference between a property that feels professionally managed and one that feels like it just happened to be clean that day.</p>

<p>Across 191 reviews and three properties at 4.93 to 4.98 stars, the cleanliness scores have held because of the checklist, not in spite of it. A cleaner without a specific list will do a good job most of the time. A cleaner with a room-by-room list will do a consistent job every time, and consistency is what the reviews measure.</p>

<p>Hand this to your cleaner before the next turnover. Adjust the specifics for your property. The goal isn't a longer list, it's a list where nothing gets missed twice.</p>
